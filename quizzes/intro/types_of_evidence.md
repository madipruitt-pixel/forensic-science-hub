### 117. Blood, semen, and saliva are analyzed primarily through:
A) Ballistics  
B) Serology  
C) Anthropology  
D) Engineering  

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**B) Serology**  
*Explanation: Serology is the study of bodily fluids to identify blood type and other markers.*
</details>

---

### 118. Handwriting analysis falls under:
A) Toxicology  
B) Questioned documents  
C) Firearms  
D) Trace evidence  

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**B) Questioned documents**  
*Explanation: Handwriting analysis is part of examining documents to verify authenticity or authorship.*
</details>

---

### 119. Explosives evidence includes:
A) Shoeprints  
B) Residues from explosions  
C) DNA  
D) Fibers  

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**B) Residues from explosions**  
*Explanation: Chemical residues from explosives help determine type, origin, and usage.*
</details>

---

### 120. Fibers are generally considered:
A) Individual evidence  
B) Class evidence  
C) Direct evidence  
D) Testimonial evidence  

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**B) Class evidence**  
*Explanation: Fibers can link a suspect to a group or location but are rarely unique to an individual.*
</details>

---

### 121. Latent fingerprints are typically:
A) Visible without processing  
B) Class evidence only  
C) Developed using specific techniques  
D) Collected through interviews  

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**C) Developed using specific techniques**  
*Explanation: Latent prints require dusting, chemical, or alternative light methods to be visualized.*
</details>

---

### 122. Glass fragments can help:
A) Determine blood type  
B) Link a suspect to a scene  
C) Identify dental records  
D) Establish motive  

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**B) Link a suspect to a scene**  
*Explanation: Broken glass or fragments can connect a suspect to a crime scene or event.*
</details>

---

### 123. Hair without a root usually provides:
A) Nuclear DNA  
B) Fingerprints  
C) Class characteristics  
D) Individual characteristics  

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**C) Class characteristics**  
*Explanation: Hair shafts without roots lack nuclear DNA and are limited to identifying species or general characteristics.*
</details>

---

### 124. Tool marks can potentially be:
A) Individual evidence  
B) Only class evidence  
C) Behavioral evidence  
D) Psychological evidence  

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**A) Individual evidence**  
*Explanation: Unique wear patterns and striations on tools can link them to specific marks left at a scene.*
</details>

---

### 125. Impressions include marks from:
A) Tires  
B) Shoes  
C) Tools  
D) All of the above  

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**D) All of the above**  
*Explanation: Impression evidence can come from tires, footwear, and tools, leaving patterns at the scene.*
</details>

---

### 126. Organs and physiological fluids are commonly analyzed in:
A) Toxicology  
B) Engineering  
C) Ballistics  
D) Anthropology  

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**A) Toxicology**  
*Explanation: Toxicology examines bodily fluids and tissues to detect drugs, alcohol, or poisons.*
</details>

---

### 127. Petroleum products such as gasoline may indicate:
A) Identity of suspect only  
B) Fire involvement  
C) Dental analysis  
D) DNA contamination  

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**B) Fire involvement**  
*Explanation: The presence of accelerants like gasoline can indicate arson or fire-related evidence.*
</details>

---

### 128. Wood and vegetative matter can:
A) Replace DNA evidence  
B) Link a suspect to a location  
C) Identify fingerprints  
D) Establish blood type  

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**B) Link a suspect to a location**  
*Explanation: Plant or wood matter can transfer to a suspect or scene, providing corroborative evidence.*
</details>
