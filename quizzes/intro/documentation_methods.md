### 69. The three primary methods of crime scene documentation are:
A) Notes, interviews, and arrest  
B) Notes, photography, and sketches  
C) Photos, fingerprints, and DNA  
D) Sketches, lab tests, and reports  

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**B) Notes, photography, and sketches**  
*Explanation: These methods ensure a thorough and permanent record of the crime scene for investigation and court purposes.*
</details>

---

### 70. Notes should be written:
A) After the case is closed  
B) From memory later  
C) Contemporaneously  
D) Only if requested  

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**C) Contemporaneously**  
*Explanation: Recording notes at the scene preserves accurate details and reduces reliance on memory.*
</details>

---

### 71. The initial walk-through allows investigators to:
A) Collect all evidence  
B) Make observations  
C) Conduct interrogations  
D) Close the case  

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**B) Make observations**  
*Explanation: The walk-through helps investigators assess the scene, plan evidence collection, and identify potential hazards.*
</details>

---

### 72. A minimum of how many types of photographs are required?
A) Two  
B) Three  
C) Four  
D) Six  

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**C) Four**  
*Explanation: Wide, mid-range, close-up with scale, and overview photos are typically taken to document the scene comprehensively.*
</details>

---

### 73. A close-up photograph with a scale is used to:
A) Show general layout  
B) Measure evidence size  
C) Identify suspects  
D) Replace sketches  

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**B) Measure evidence size**  
*Explanation: Including a scale ensures accurate size and proportion documentation for evidentiary purposes.*
</details>
