# 🔬 Individual vs. Class Characteristics

Welcome to the **Individual vs. Class Characteristics** quiz! Click on each question to reveal the answer and a brief explanation.

---

## Individual Characteristics

### 101. Individual characteristics are best described as:
A) Common within a population  
B) Unique to a specific source  
C) Based on eyewitness accounts  
D) Easily duplicated  

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**B) Unique to a specific source**  
*Explanation: Individual characteristics allow forensic scientists to link evidence to one specific person or object.*
</details>

---

### 102. Which of the following is considered individual evidence?
A) Blood type  
B) Paint color  
C) Fingerprint ridge detail  
D) Fiber color  

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**C) Fingerprint ridge detail**  
*Explanation: Fingerprint ridges have unique patterns that distinguish one individual from all others.*
</details>

---

### 103. Unique striations on a bullet are an example of:
A) Class characteristics  
B) Circumstantial evidence  
C) Individual characteristics  
D) Biological evidence  

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**C) Individual characteristics**  
*Explanation: Striations on bullets are unique marks caused by the barrel, allowing identification of a specific firearm.*
</details>

---

### 104. The probability of two individuals sharing identical individual characteristics is:
A) High  
B) Moderate  
C) Extremely low  
D) Equal  

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**C) Extremely low**  
*Explanation: True individual characteristics are highly unlikely to be identical in two separate sources.*
</details>

---

### 105. DNA analysis strengthens identification by establishing:
A) Class traits only  
B) Individual characteristics  
C) Blood type groups  
D) Shoe size  

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**B) Individual characteristics**  
*Explanation: DNA is unique to each person (except identical twins), providing strong evidence of identity.*
</details>

---

### 106. Determining individualization can involve:
A) Pure mathematics only  
B) Subjective judgment  
C) Random guessing  
D) Jury decision  

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**B) Subjective judgment**  
*Explanation: Forensic examiners may rely on expert interpretation alongside objective data when identifying unique traits.*
</details>

---

### 107. The number of characteristics required for individualization is:
A) Universally agreed upon  
B) Fixed by law  
C) Still debated  
D) Exactly twelve  

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**C) Still debated**  
*Explanation: There is no universal standard; courts and forensic communities debate the number of features needed.*
</details>

## Class Characteristics

### 108. Class characteristics associate evidence with:
A) A specific person  
B) A single tool  
C) A group  
D) A laboratory  

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**C) A group**  
*Explanation: Class characteristics link evidence to a category or type, not a single individual.*
</details>

---

### 109. Blood type is considered:
A) Individual evidence  
B) Class evidence  
C) Direct evidence  
D) Circumstantial only  

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**B) Class evidence**  
*Explanation: Blood type can exclude some people but cannot uniquely identify a single individual.*
</details>

---

### 110. Paint chips of similar color typically provide:
A) Individual characteristics  
B) Class characteristics  
C) DNA identification  
D) Behavioral profiling  

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**B) Class characteristics**  
*Explanation: Paint chips match a general source or type but cannot point to one specific item without further analysis.*
</details>

---

### 111. The product rule is used to:
A) Multiply fingerprints  
B) Increase probability using multiple factors  
C) Determine guilt  
D) Identify suspects visually  

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**B) Increase probability using multiple factors**  
*Explanation: The product rule combines probabilities of independent characteristics to strengthen identification.*
</details>

---

### 112. Class evidence is encountered in investigations:
A) Less often than individual evidence  
B) More often than individual evidence  
C) Only in homicides  
D) Only in burglaries  

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**B) More often than individual evidence**  
*Explanation: Most evidence is class evidence, such as fibers or paint, which is common to multiple sources.*
</details>

---

### 113. Multiple pieces of class evidence found together can:
A) Become irrelevant  
B) Increase evidentiary value  
C) Replace DNA  
D) Guarantee conviction  

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**B) Increase evidentiary value**  
*Explanation: Correlating multiple class characteristics can narrow down potential sources significantly.*
</details>
