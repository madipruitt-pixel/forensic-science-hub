### 79. Cross-transfer evidence includes materials such as:
A) Confessions  
B) Hairs and fibers  
C) Warrants  
D) Reports  

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**B) Hairs and fibers**  
*Explanation: Cross-transfer evidence occurs when materials like hair, fibers, or soil are transferred between people, objects, or locations.*
</details>

### 80. In burglary investigations, investigators often focus on:
A) Blood spatter  
B) Dental records  
C) Tool marks  
D) Toxicology  

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**C) Tool marks**  
*Explanation: Tool marks can link the instrument used in a burglary to the suspect, making them a key focus in property crimes.*
</details>

### 81. Vacuuming critical areas is used to collect:
A) Firearms  
B) Trace evidence  
C) Fingerprints  
D) DNA databases  

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**B) Trace evidence**  
*Explanation: Vacuum collection can capture small particles like fibers, hair, or soil that may be overlooked by hand collection.*
</details>

---

### 82. Fingernail scrapings may provide:
A) Weather data  
B) Trace evidence  
C) Structural damage  
D) Ballistics  

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**B) Trace evidence**  
*Explanation: Scrapings from under fingernails can contain skin, hair, or fibers from a struggle, which are considered trace evidence.*
</details>

---

### 83. DNA may be collected from:
A) Saliva  
B) Sweat  
C) Skin cells  
D) All of the above  

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
**D) All of the above**  
*Explanation: DNA can be recovered from many biological sources including saliva, sweat, and skin cells, making all options correct.*
</details>
